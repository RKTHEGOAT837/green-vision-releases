Where Should a City Plant Its Next Ten Thousand Trees?

Every Indian city plants trees. Almost none can say why it chose that spot. We built an app that reads the air, the canopy and the soil of a place — then designs and costs the planting, down to the last sapling.

Why it exists

Ahmedabad loses tree cover every year, and nobody could say where it was going fastest. Most greening tools stop at a colourful heat map. Green Vision carries the answer through to the next question: which plot, which species, and what will it cost?

What it does

Click anywhere and it draws a 100 km² perimeter, reading only what is inside — air quality at nine points, canopy from satellite, heat, rainfall, and every building, road and park from a map index held on your own computer. A read comes back in under a second. Five cities ship with real data: Ahmedabad, Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru and Chennai, 632 hexagonal cells with 42 months of history each.

A traffic panel finds the bottlenecks where dust and heat collect. The Studio lets you draw a plot and place trees ranked against that site’s own soil, with paths, water, benches, lighting and play equipment. Cost builds in rupees as you work, into a full bill of quantities — the tax basis and the tree guard are yours to state rather than the app’s to assume.

A twelve-check review then scores the design out of 100 and says what is wrong with it: trees too close together to grow, too little shade over a path, too few benches. The 3D builder reconstructs the real buildings, roads and water around a site. Finished work goes to a shared Library, while your own History stays private and follows you between devices.

A figure you cannot trace is worse than no figure at all.

Who it is for

Municipal corporations and ward officers who must justify where a planting budget goes; planners and landscape designers who need a costed drawing rather than a heat map; schools studying their own neighbourhood; and anyone who has wondered why their street has one tree on it.

Where it stands

Every number carries a chip saying Measured, Modelled or Assumed. Green Vision has reached the National round of the India AI Impact Festival 2026. It runs on Windows, works offline, and is free.
